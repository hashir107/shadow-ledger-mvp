Details below ↓

Today the same capital events are turned into accounting entries several times, by different people in different tools. In v1, the platform generates those entries once, with a full trail. A controller approves them, and the accountant imports them into DATEV instead of retyping. For the fund administrator, this lowers the cost of serving each fund. For customers, it means a faster, more reliable closing and reporting.

Assumptions: one closed-ended fund in Germany, with an external accountant keeping the books in DATEV. The platform already holds capital call and distribution data and receives bank transactions.

1Problem Setup

There is no single trusted version of how an event became a booking, so ops re-key data, the accountant reinterprets it, and the controller reconciles two sources.

PersonaPain todayGood outcome
Fund administrator: ops team (primary user) and managementOps re-key events into spreadsheets for the accountant and chase mismatches; ops cost grows with each fund, with little view of close status across fundsEntries are generated automatically and ops handle only exceptions; lower cost to serve, with one view of close progress and errors
Fund controller / CFO (approver)Can't see the books until the accountant has posted; reconciles two sourcesReviews and approves in one place; trusts the numbers
External accountant (receives the output)Gets spreadsheets or PDFs, interprets them and posts by handA balanced import file in their own chart of accounts, traceable to source

Regulators, e.g. BaFin (not a v1 user)Expect proper bookkeeping, retention and accurate reporting (rules to confirm with Legal)Complete, unaltered, retained records that tie to reports

v1 is designed for ops and the controller. The others benefit indirectly: the audit trail and append-only records serve auditors and regulators from day one. The accountant decides whether it worked: if they still retype, we haven't solved the problem.

v1 scope: one fund and three event types: capital calls (the call and the LP payment), management fees (charged and paid) and distributions. They're the core of fund life, the platform already holds their data, and they're rule-based.

Outputs: journals, a per-LP sub-ledger, a fund trial balance, review and approval, a DATEV import file, and reconciliation against the bank.

Deliberately out of v1: other fund expenses (they need invoice data, VAT rules and accruals; first in line for v1.5), FX, carry and waterfall calculations, NAV and valuations, multiple funds or jurisdictions, financial statements and tax, two-way DATEV sync, and auto-approval.

Boundary: the platform is the sub-ledger, DATEV stays the general ledger of record.

In one sentence: the platform owns the repetitive, rule-based translation of events into entries, plus the why behind each entry. DATEV and the accountant keep everything that needs professional judgement or is legally theirs to sign. This is a starting line, not a ceiling: as event coverage, controls and accountants' trust grow, the platform can become the general ledger of record itself, with DATEV as an export target rather than where the books live.

2Framing

  • What I'd ask to see (last 2–4 quarters of the flagship fund):
    • from DATEV: the booking journal, trial balance and chart of accounts
    • the spreadsheets and emails ops send the accountant, and the questions that come back
    • call and distribution notices, fee calculations and bank statements
    • the close calendar
  • Manual work: trace 10–20 events from source to booking, counting touches, handoffs and minutes per event. Sit in on one quarter-end close.
  • Errors: match platform events to DATEV postings on amount, date and account. Sort mismatches by type (missing, timing, amount, wrong account, duplicate) and fix the biggest category first.
  • Close time: days from period end to sign-off, and where the waiting happens.
  • Business link: fewer ops hours per fund (the platform grows funds faster than headcount), a faster close and faster LP reporting, fewer corrections and more trust, and a structured ledger as the base for later reporting (capital accounts, NAV).

Sample workflow: the Shadow Ledger prototype is a clickable mock-up of the workflow, with invented data. Example Fund I has two LPs (60/40), and its Q1 includes a €1m capital call, a €50k management fee and a €200k distribution. The prototype shows:

  • platform and bank events becoming journal entries through versioned rules
  • four-eyes approval
  • a DATEV-style export batch and reconciliation
  • LP capital accounts and a balanced trial balance (Dr 1,000,000 = Cr 1,000,000)
  • four exception scenarios: a duplicate bank line, a missing LP split, a correction after export (reversal plus replacement) and a manual change in DATEV

3Strategic Approach

Earn the accountant's trust on a few events, prove the time saved, then scale. The fund administrator delivers fund admin as a service, so every manual accounting hour is partly its own cost. The Shadow Ledger is first a margin lever, then a faster, clearer close for customers, and later the base for reporting.

Principles

  • Correct books first. One wrong export costs more trust than ten manual entries save, hence shadow mode and review/approval.
  • Every entry traces back. Each one links to its source event, rule version and export batch. No entry exists without a source.
  • Append-only. Approved or exported entries are never edited. A correction is a reversal plus a new, linked entry. Finance should confirm the specific German audit requirements.
  • A standard core, flexible at the edges. Every fund shares one event model and ledger. Fund-specific needs (chart of accounts, policies, reports) are met through configuration first, and through bounded, versioned extensions where configuration isn't enough. Mapping rules stay owned by Finance and signed off by the accountant. Needs that keep coming back are folded into the standard product.
  • Review exceptions, not everything. Clean entries are batch-approved, and automation is earned: auto-approval only after a full quarter with no corrections.
CaseHandling
Duplicate eventA unique key (source ID + bank reference) flags the second copy instead of posting it

Review and approval: Draft → Needs review (only if flagged) → Approved → Exported → Reconciled. Ops prepare entries and the fund controller approves; nobody approves their own entries. A rejected entry goes back to Draft with a reason, and a failed export goes back to Approved once it's fixed. Every change records who, when and why.

Quick win on the real foundation: a DATEV import for capital calls saves visible time for ops and the accountant within one quarter. It's built on the real event and rule model, just narrowly, so we never ship a throwaway script.

Leading indicators tell us early whether v1 is working. Lagging indicators prove the business value after a full quarter, and over time should show in retention of funds on the ledger. All are baselined in discovery. Communicating: CFOs and controllers get a one-page close summary each quarter, in plain language. It covers events processed, how many needed a person, what was corrected and why, and close days compared with last quarter. Accountants get a discrepancy list, each item linked to its source.

5Risks & Tradeoffs

RiskMitigation
Biggest risk: wrong entries that look right (bad mapping, automating too early)Accountant signs off the mapping; shadow mode before go-live; four-eyes approval; reversals; auto-approval only after a clean quarter
Poor or late source data (e.g. unclear bank references)Validate on ingest; hold incomplete events; report data quality back to ops and fund managers
The accountant doesn't adopt it (extra work, or a threat to their role)Design it with them, in their chart of accounts; importing must be less work than typing
Customer-specific charts and policiesA standard core with per-fund configuration; bounded extensions where needed, reviewed so repeat needs become product features
Audit, retention and liabilityAppend-only records and audit log; confirm with Finance and Legal; the accountant keeps the final sign-off
Accounting expertise is scarceA fixed weekly slot with a Finance expert or the accountant from day one

Tradeoffs I'm choosing: correctness over coverage; human review over speed in v1; a sub-ledger over a full ledger. If discovery shows accountants won't import entries, a structured data export is the fallback.
