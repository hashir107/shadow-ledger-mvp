Details below ↓

Today the same capital events are turned into accounting entries several times, by different people in different tools. In v1, the platform generates those entries once, with a full trail. Fund Admin approves them, and the accountant imports them into DATEV instead of retyping. For the fund administrator, this lowers the cost of serving each fund. For customers, it means a faster, more reliable closing and reporting.

Assumptions: one closed-ended fund in Germany, with an external accountant keeping the books in DATEV. The platform already holds capital call and distribution data and receives bank transactions.

1Problem Setup

There is no single trusted version of how an event became a booking, so Fund Admin re-keys data and reconciles two sources, while the accountant reinterprets what arrives.

v1 is designed for Fund Admin. The others benefit indirectly: the audit trail and append-only records serve auditors and regulators from day one. The accountant decides whether it worked: if they still retype, we haven't solved the problem.

v1 scope: one fund and three event types: capital calls (the call and the LP payment), management fees (charged and paid) and distributions. They're the core of fund life, the platform already holds their data, and they're rule-based.

Outputs: journals, a fund trial balance, review and approval, a DATEV import file, and reconciliation against the bank.

Deliberately out of v1: the per-LP sub-ledger and capital accounts, other fund expenses (they need invoice data, VAT rules and accruals; first in line for v1.5), FX, carry and waterfall calculations, NAV and valuations, multiple funds or jurisdictions, financial statements and tax, two-way DATEV sync, and auto-approval.

Boundary: the platform is the sub-ledger, DATEV stays the general ledger of record.

In one sentence: the platform owns the repetitive, rule-based translation of events into entries, plus the why behind each entry. DATEV and the accountant keep everything that needs professional judgement or is legally theirs to sign. This is a starting line, not a ceiling: as event coverage, controls and accountants' trust grow, the platform can become the general ledger of record itself, with DATEV as an export target rather than where the books live.

2Framing

  • What I'd ask to see (last 2–4 quarters of the flagship fund):
    • from DATEV: the booking journal, trial balance and chart of accounts
    • the spreadsheets and emails Fund Admin sends the accountant, and the questions that come back
    • call and distribution notices, fee calculations and bank statements
    • the close calendar
  • Manual work: trace 10–20 events from source document to posting, counting touches, handoffs and minutes per event. Sit in on one quarter-end close.
  • Errors: match platform events to DATEV postings on amount, date and account. Classify reconciling items by type (missing, timing, amount, wrong account, duplicate) and fix the biggest category first.
  • Close time: days from period end to sign-off, and where the waiting happens.
  • Commercial Alignment: fewer Fund Admin hours per fund (so funds can grow faster than headcount), a faster close and faster LP reporting, fewer corrections and more trust, and a structured ledger as the base for later reporting (capital accounts, NAV). Priority: rank it against other roadmap work by Fund Admin hours saved and customer impact; the discovery baseline decides where it lands.

Sample workflow: the Shadow Ledger prototype is a clickable mock-up of the workflow, with invented data. Example Fund I's Q1 includes a €1m capital call, a €50k management fee and a €200k distribution. The prototype shows:

  • platform and bank events becoming journal entries through transparent mapping rules: each shows its trigger, debit and credit accounts, the reason, and the entries it produced
  • four-eyes approval
  • a DATEV-style export batch and reconciliation
  • a balanced trial balance (Dr = Cr)
  • three exception scenarios: a duplicate bank line, a correction after export (reversal plus a correcting entry) and a manual change in DATEV

3Strategic Approach

Earn the accountant's trust on a few events, prove the time saved, then scale. Fund administration is delivered as a service, so every manual accounting hour is partly the administrator's own cost. The Shadow Ledger is first a margin lever, then a faster, clearer close for customers, and later the base for reporting.

Principles

  • Correct books first. One wrong export costs more trust than ten manual entries save, hence shadow mode and review/approval.
  • Every entry traces back. Each one links to its source event, rule version and export batch. No entry exists without a source.
  • Append-only. Approved or exported entries are never edited. A correction is a reversing entry plus a new, linked correcting entry. Finance should confirm the specific German audit requirements.
  • A standard core, flexible at the edges. One event model and ledger for every fund; fund-specific charts, policies and reports through configuration first, bounded versioned extensions second. Finance owns the mapping rules, the accountant signs them off, and repeat needs become standard product.
  • Review exceptions, not everything. Clean entries are batch-approved, and automation is earned: auto-approval only after a full quarter with no corrections.

Event types and their mapping to accounts

Each event type has one mapping rule: what triggers it, which account it debits and which it credits, where the amount comes from, and why. Rules are configuration, not code, so a new event type is a new rule. v1 has five:

Capital call issuedLP receivablesLP capital contributions

Corrections, back-dated events and reversals

Approved or exported entries are never edited: every fix is a reversing entry plus a linked correcting entry, so the trail stays complete. Each case:

CaseHandling
Duplicate eventA unique key (source ID + bank reference) flags the second copy instead of posting it
Missing data (e.g. a bank payment that can't be matched to a call)Held in Needs review with a reason; never guessed
Wrong mappingFix the rule as a new version; identify affected entries; reverse and re-post through approval
Back-dated (prior-period) eventOpen period: post with the original value date. Closed or exported period: post as a prior-period adjustment in the current period, referencing the original document
Failed exportValidated before export (debits equal credits, accounts exist in the chart of accounts, posting period open); all or nothing; safe retry
Reconciliation differencePlatform vs DATEV trial balance at period end, plus bank statement vs cash ledger (bank reconciliation); each reconciling item gets an owner

Review and approval before export

Draft → Needs review (only if flagged) → Approved → Exported → Reconciled. One Fund Admin prepares entries and another approves; nobody approves their own entries. Clean entries are approved as a batch, so attention goes to the flagged ones. A rejected entry goes back to Draft with a reason, and a failed export goes back to Approved once it's fixed. Every change records who, when and why.

Quick wins vs foundational work

An early DATEV import for capital calls saves visible time for Fund Admin and the accountant. It's built on the real event and rule model, just narrowly, so we never ship a throwaway script.
